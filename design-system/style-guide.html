UI guidelines

Navigation and information architecture

  • Guest site: a top bar only (search, account menu), no sidebar, because the guest journey is a short linear funnel (search → room → book → confirm) that a sidebar would clutter.
  • Back office: a persistent left sidebar with top-level sections Reservations, Rooms & Rates, Guests, Billing, and Reports, because staff jump between these sections all day and a sidebar keeps them one click away during a live check-in queue.
  • The back office always shows a top bar with the current user and property name, because more than one property may exist in future and staff must never be unsure which one they're editing.

Layout and density

  • The back office is data-dense (compact rows, tables over cards) because front-desk staff scan many reservations at once, often on a shared desktop monitor.
  • The guest site is spacious (cards, generous spacing) because it is a consumer, trust-building purchase flow, not a work tool.
  • Back office layouts assume a minimum 1280px-wide desktop/tablet viewport, because front-desk PCs and tablets are the only devices staff use.
  • The guest site must work down to a 375px mobile viewport, because guests frequently book from their phone.

Component usage

  • Use .card for anything a guest browses and chooses between (room types, rate options, confirmation summaries), because each is a self-contained, comparable unit.
  • Use a table (not .card) for any back-office list a staff member scans, sorts, or bulk-acts on (reservations, arrivals/departures, invoices), because rows let staff compare many records at a glance.
  • Use .chip for status (confirmed, checked-in, refunded, overdue) wherever a reservation, payment, or invoice is listed, because status is the first thing staff and guests look for.
  • Use .button-primary for the one key action per screen (Book now, Check in, Save rate); use .button-secondary for alternate actions; reserve .button-danger for destructive, hard-to-undo actions (cancel booking, void invoice), because a single clear primary action avoids hesitation at the moment of commitment or during a busy front-desk shift.
  • Use .field and .input for every form control across both apps, because booking and back-office data entry must look and behave identically to feel like one product.

Theme

  • Light theme only, for both apps, because the back office runs on front-desk PCs under normal office lighting and the guest site prioritizes trust and legibility over a dark aesthetic.

Voice and tone

  • Guest-facing copy is warm and reassuring, plain language, no jargon (e.g. "Your room is booked" not "Reservation created"), because guests are making a purchase and may be anxious about cancellation or payment.
  • Back-office copy is terse and operational (e.g. "Room 204 — checked in 14:32"), because staff read it quickly, often mid-conversation with a guest at the desk.
  • Error messages always state what to do next (e.g. "Card declined — try another card or pay at the desk"), because both guests and staff need to resolve the issue immediately, not just be told it failed.
